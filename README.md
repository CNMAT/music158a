# Music158a Fall 2026 INTRODUCTION

<sub>[*(Jump to Fall 2026 Music 158a SYLLABUS)*](_SYLLABUS_158a_Fall_2026.md)<sub>

What do we mean when we say something is "musical"? 

How can computers and AI assistants deepen our understanding of what we call "musical"? <br />

![gif](images/rhythmic_activity_pulsing_dots.gif)

How can computational systems designed for musical control expand the ways humans think about and make music with machines? <br />

This course explores musical creativity with machines by making musical things.

It is OK not to know things. Some of us are musicians, some programmers, some both. Share what you know and ask about what you do not. Depend on your classmates and teachers. Please ask! <br />

We do not ask whether an audible artifact/outcome is music or whether we like it. We ask what is musical about a behavior and how, through iteration and thoughtful control, we can make it more musical. We do not ask what is best, just what is better. <br />

![gif](images/rhythmic_prob_full16_4beats.gif)

How do we decide which is better?  

![gif](images/rhythmic_prob_full16_4beats_odd_only_fast.gif)

![gif](images/rhythmic_activity_sweep-fade.gif)

<br />This course is not centered on imitating conventional music-making, styles, or genres. Instead, we replace reliance on familiarity with inquisitiveness, openness, and testing through audible feedback and comparison. Computational systems can extend beyond established human practices toward an expanded field of human and machine "musicality." The question remains: is it musical, and how?<br />

Rather than pursuing fully autonomous music generation, we focus on collaborative, semi-autonomous systems. Using decision engines, state machines, AI coding support, CNMAT's history of computer music, and auditory feedback, students develop new forms of personal musical expression with machines. <br />

What forms of musical thought become possible when sound, structure, and process are treated as computable materials—some extending beyond the limits of human perception, performance, and cognition? 

When does an algorithm become a compositional partner, model, or musical agent? 

What kinds of musical knowledge are made explicit when we formalize them computationally? 

What musical knowledge becomes explicit when formalized computationally—and what resists formalization?

How do feedback, randomness, recursion, emergence, and constraint function musically?
